Graduation Thesis

Sensor Fusion and Spatial-Accuracy-Based Prediction, Risk Analysis and Collision Avoidance in Autonomous Vehicles

İstanbul Technical University · Department of Geomatics Engineering · June 2026
Advisor: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Caner Güney

This thesis evaluates how reliably an autonomous vehicle can perceive its surroundings and avoid collisions under changing weather. Using the CARLA 0.9.16 simulator on the Town10HD urban map, the positional accuracy and detection performance of six sensors — GNSS, IMU, a 128-channel LiDAR, radar, a YOLOv8n camera and a depth camera — were measured over 24,000 frames per condition (sunny, rainy and foggy).

Sensor placement and reference frame of the simulated vehicle.

Approach

  • Localization: Extended Kalman Filter fusing GNSS and IMU for ego pose.
  • Detection: LiDAR–radar late-fusion for surrounding vehicles.
  • Prediction: residual-learning LSTM for 3 s trajectory forecasting.
  • Risk: Time-to-Collision (TTC) analysis, 20 m range, 2 s threshold.

Key results

  • GNSS position RMSE ≈ 0.16 m, virtually weather-independent.
  • Radar RMSE 1.24–1.27 m across all conditions — weather-resilient.
  • LiDAR–radar fusion expanded detection coverage by +117% (sunny).
  • LSTM cut trajectory error (ADE) by 18.6–25.4% vs. a constant-velocity baseline; best case 0.69 m ADE / 0.72 m FDE.
  • Zero collisions recorded across every session.
